# Phase-3 figure captions (EMNLP submission)

Source-of-truth captions for the five Phase-3 figures. Each section pairs a paper-grade caption (2–5 sentences, complete on its own) with an accessibility alt-text line. Captions are authored in Markdown for in-repo readability; before pasting into the paper LaTeX `\caption{}` blocks, convert the Markdown inline formatting (backticks → `\texttt{}`, `**bold**` → `\textbf{}`, `_italics_` → `\emph{}`) — the prose itself ports verbatim.

All figures share these conventions: two-column ACL/EMNLP width (~3.15 in single, ~6.30 in double), Times serif body via `scienceplots` `science` style, Okabe-Ito colorblind-safe categorical palette, RdBu_r diverging with `TwoSlopeNorm` at p95 (no long-tail saturation), language order on every axis is **resource-tier descending: en → es → zh → ur**, ISO codes only on tick labels (native script reserved for captions and side tables). All figures are emitted as both PDF (paper) and PNG (preview/blog).

Per-cell grain disclosure: every figure that plots individual cells uses the `condition × seed × template × benchmark × data × instr` grain from the HF `refined-tables/cells.tsv` (or `conclusion_flips.tsv`). The `(condition × benchmark)` aggregate counts reported in §8.3 of the analysis writeup are coarsenings of these per-cell grids.

## Figure 1 — Extractor effect: condition vs. baseline, original vs. refined scoring

**Caption.** Two-panel slopegraph of `mean Δ-vs-baseline` for each (condition × instruction-language) cell under the original strict-English extractor (left endpoint) and the refined extractor (right endpoint). Each panel aggregates per-cell deltas across seed, template, and data-language, yielding 28 lines per panel. Panel (a) shows SIB-200 on a ±45 pp y-axis; panel (b) shows XNLI on a ±10 pp y-axis — **the two panels use different scales by necessity and should be read on their own axes**. Line colour encodes the orig→refined transition for that condition × instruction-language: vermillion = win→loss flip, blue = loss→win flip, orange = deflated (same sign, smaller magnitude), green/red = stable positive/negative, gray = near-zero. The dashed horizontal reference at y = 0 marks baseline parity. Twelve SIB-200 lines cross from positive to negative under the refined scoring (vermillion) — the cond-2 / cond-3 SIB-200 advantage that §8.4 documents as Rule-A over-credit. XNLI shows only stable-positive lines (no flips), confirming that the XNLI tier ladder is purely additive (it resolves previously-rejected native answers but does not re-route already-credited ones). Data: `refined-tables/vs_baseline_cells.tsv`.

**Alt text.** Two-panel slopegraph of mean Δ-vs-baseline under original vs refined extractor: SIB-200 on a ±45 pp scale shows 12 win→loss flips concentrated in condition-2 / condition-3; XNLI on a ±10 pp scale shows only stable-positive lines.

## Figure 2 — Cell-level Δ structure across 1,664 per-cell scores

**Caption.** Single-panel scatter of every (condition × seed × template × benchmark × data × instr) cell in the Phase-3 evaluation grid (n = 1,664). X-axis: `Δparse-failure rate` (refined − original, percentage points; negative = parse-failures resolved). Y-axis: `Δaccuracy` (percentage points; positive = the refined extractor read correct answers the original refused). Colour encodes benchmark: SIB-200 vermillion, XNLI blue, X-CSQA green, Belebele orange. Marker alpha = 0.70 for SIB-200 and XNLI; alpha = 0.25 for X-CSQA and Belebele (whose cells cluster at the origin because the refined extractor did not touch their letter-answer format — they appear as a faint cloud rather than a visual focus). The dashed reference lines at x = 0 and y = 0 divide the plane into four quadrants; the upper-left "extractor lift" quadrant holds 215 cells (parse-failures recovered with the recovered rows mostly being correct), the lower-right "regression" quadrant holds 106 cells (the cond-2-ur Rule-A correction surface). Four extreme cells are annotated: `cond-5 ur/5k SIB-200 es→ur t2` (−76 pp pf, +51 pp acc — the single biggest extractor lift); `cond-2 ur/20k SIB-200 zh→ur t1` (+43 pp pf, −14 pp acc — the largest single regression); `cond-2 ur/20k SIB-200 en→ur t1` (+36 pp pf, −12 pp acc); and `cond-5 zh/5k XNLI es→zh t2` (−66 pp pf, +22 pp acc — the headline XNLI Tier-3 paraphrase recovery). The strong anti-correlation along the negative diagonal is mechanical: parse-failure recoveries become accuracy lifts when the recovered rows are correct. Data: `refined-tables/cells.tsv`.

**Alt text.** Per-cell scatter of Δparse-failure (x) vs Δaccuracy (y) across 1,664 cells, coloured by benchmark, with four extreme cells annotated; X-CSQA and Belebele cluster at the origin; strong anti-correlation diagonal from upper-left to lower-right.

## Figure 3 — Regression concentration: where the 106 SIB-200 regressions come from

**Caption.** Two-panel figure documenting the per-condition distribution of SIB-200 accuracy regressions under the refined extractor (Δacc < 0; n = 106 across 12 conditions). **(a)** Horizontal bar chart sorted by regression count descending. The `condition-2-ur-5k` bar (38 of 106) is rendered in saturated vermillion to mark the concentration; the remaining 11 conditions are in neutral gray. Eleven of the twelve conditions contribute fewer than half as many regressions as `condition-2-ur-5k`. **(b)** Heatmap of mean Δacc within `condition-2-ur-5k`, with `(data_lang → instr_lang)` on the y-axis (language order: en → es → zh → ur) and template on the x-axis; colormap RdBu_r centred at zero with ±7 pp endpoints, each cell annotated with its mean Δacc in percentage points. The pattern is asymmetric across templates: template-1 is brutal across every `instr=ur` pair (en→ur −7.3 pp, es→ur −7.0 pp, zh→ur −6.2 pp, ur→ur −5.1 pp), while template-2 catastrophic regressions concentrate on the matched `ur → ur` diagonal (−4.1 pp). Mechanism (§4): PR #49 had introduced a `سیاست/تکنالوجی → science/technology` Rule-A mapping (4 cases correct, 70 wrong on that string); the Urdu-tuned model emits the compound frequently, so removing the over-credit moves these cells from false-positive accuracy to honest parse-failure. Data: `refined-tables/cells.tsv`.

**Alt text.** Two-panel figure: (a) horizontal bar chart of SIB-200 regression counts per condition with condition-2-ur-5k bar at 38 in saturated vermillion and 11 other conditions in gray, and (b) heatmap of condition-2-ur-5k mean Δacc by (data→instr) × template using RdBu_r centred at zero with ±7 pp endpoints.

## Figure 4 — Sign-flip slopegraphs (three views, one finding)

**Caption.** Three slopegraphs documenting the 48 cells whose `sign(Δ-vs-baseline)` changed between the original and refined extractors. All three panels plot per-cell deltas (not aggregates) at `(condition × seed × template × benchmark × data × instr)` granularity, with row labels following the pattern `<benchmark> · t<template> · <data>→<instr> · <condition> · s<seed>` and lines coloured by verdict (vermillion = win → loss, blue = loss → win). The dashed gray reference at y = 0 marks baseline parity; crossing the reference is the sign-flip event the figure isolates. **(4a) `fig04_signflip_sib200_top15`** — main paper figure: the 15 SIB-200 flips with the largest `|shift|`. 13 are win → loss and 2 are loss → win; the 12 highest-magnitude lines are `condition-2-ur-{5k,20k}` cells, and the single most extreme is `cond-2-ur-20k SIB-200 zh→ur t1 s42` with `|shift| = 45.6 pp` (delta_orig + 32.4 pp → delta_rep − 13.2 pp). **(4b) `fig04_signflip_sib200_all43`** — appendix figure with the same conventions as (4a), plotting all 43 SIB-200 flips sorted by `|shift|` descending in a taller frame (figsize height ≈ 9 in). **(4c) `fig04_signflip_xnli_all5`** — small standalone figure for the 5 XNLI flips. All five are `loss → win` (blue), all on `condition-1-en-*` (English-only fine-tunes), all template-2, all `instr=zh`, magnitudes +2.5 pp to +9.2 pp. The XNLI flips are the **mirror-image of the SIB-200 deflations** (§8.4): where the original extractor over-credited fine-tuned models on Rule-A SIB-200 bug cells, it _under-credited_ condition-1 on XNLI-zh Tier-3 paraphrases (`直接结果`, `否定`, `没有.{0,8}关系`) that the refined extractor's tier ladder now resolves. Data: `refined-tables/conclusion_flips.tsv`.

**Alt text.** Three slopegraphs showing the 48 conclusion-flips: top-15 SIB-200 flips dominated by condition-2-ur win→loss lines in vermillion, all 43 SIB-200 flips for the appendix, and all 5 XNLI flips in blue (loss→win on condition-1-en-* template-2 instr=zh).

## Figure 5 — Cond-5 rehabilitated: SIB-200 collapse halved, X-CSQA loss is real

**Caption.** Slopegraph of the four "loud cells" for `cond-5` (the Aya-translated training mix), comparing each cell's mean Δ-vs-baseline under the original extractor (left endpoint) and the refined extractor (right endpoint). One line per `(condition × benchmark)` pair; the dashed gray reference at y = 0 marks baseline parity. The two SIB-200 lines (Okabe-Ito green, "extractor-rehabilitated") move substantially toward zero under refined scoring: `cond-5-zh-5k SIB-200` moves from −24.5 pp to −12.8 pp (annotated "halved"), and `cond-5-ur-5k SIB-200` moves from −6.6 pp to −1.4 pp. The two X-CSQA lines (Okabe-Ito sky-blue, "unchanged") are flat under both extractors: `cond-5-zh-5k X-CSQA` at −2.0 pp and `cond-5-ur-5k X-CSQA` at −4.5 pp. The reading: cond-5's apparent SIB-200 collapse was largely an artefact of the original extractor refusing the model's native-script answers (a coverage problem, not a training-data problem); the X-CSQA degradation persists across both extractors and is therefore a real model effect of the Aya-translated training mix on commonsense QA. Data: `refined-tables/vs_baseline_by_cond_x_bench.tsv`.

**Alt text.** Slopegraph of four cond-5 condition × benchmark pairs: two SIB-200 lines in green move from −24.5 pp / −6.6 pp toward zero under refined scoring; two X-CSQA lines in sky-blue stay flat at −2.0 pp and −4.5 pp under both extractors.
